# Visual language

This is the non-negotiable art direction. The kit can span tropical ruins,
volcanic industry, lunar colonies, luxury towers, and neon streets because all
of them obey the same visual grammar.

## Shape grammar

- Start with a **strong primary silhouette**: one readable mass, one service
  mass, and one focal signal. Avoid evenly noisy silhouettes.
- Use a 1 m construction grid. Structural increments are 0.25 m, 0.5 m, 1 m,
  2 m, and 4 m wherever possible. A one-off dimension needs a documented
  reason.
- Human-scale openings are 1.0–1.2 m wide and 2.2–2.6 m high. Handrails sit at
  1.05 m. Main equipment is designed around a 1.8 m operator.
- Primary edges use a 0.04–0.08 m chamfer at human scale. Hero machinery may
  reach 0.12 m. Do not bevel every micro-edge; preserve graphic planes.
- Use rounded rectangles, shallow trapezoids, clipped circles, and purposeful
  triangular braces. Avoid arbitrary sci-fi greebles and unexplained spikes.
- Repeated elements should expose a rhythm: bolt pair, panel seam, vent slot,
  cable loop, or light bar. Repetition is a design signal, not filler.
- A prop should have a front or operational face, a service face, and a back.
  The back may be simpler, but it must still explain mounting, cooling, or
  access.

## Three-layer construction

1. **Chassis** — load-bearing shell, frame, feet, wall, or foundation. This
   establishes scale and ownership.
2. **Service layer** — panels, vents, pipes, cables, hinges, latches, access
   seams, drains, and maintenance labels. This explains function.
3. **Signal layer** — lights, screens, holograms, warning stripes, color blocks,
   foliage, graffiti, or damage. This communicates state and place.

The service layer should never float without a bracket, gland, clip, or recessed
channel. The signal layer should never carry the entire silhouette unless the
asset is explicitly a sign, beacon, or energy device.

## Value and visual hierarchy

At gameplay distance, the value hierarchy is:

1. navigable floor and cover;
2. interactable edge and opening;
3. landmark silhouette;
4. state signal;
5. close-up surface detail.

Use dark service cavities to separate light shells. Keep emissive elements
thin, grouped, and directional. A prop with more than two competing emissive
colors needs a specific hero justification.

## Material rhythm

The kit's unifying rhythm is **matte shell → dark recess → precise accent**.
Materials are selected from the central library, never invented per prop. Most
assets use two to four materials in the first pass and gain additional surface
variation through masks, decals, and damage—not by adding random shaders.

## Lighting and atmosphere

- Base scene lighting is neutral-to-cool; warm practicals describe occupancy,
  heat, or warning.
- Emission is a communication channel. Cyan means active navigation/data,
  magenta means phase/energy anomaly, amber means caution/service, red means
  critical danger, and green means biological/approved safe state.
- Keep bloom localized. The light source must be visible or physically implied
  by a lens, tube, panel seam, or projector.
- Wet streets, ice, lava, and phase effects change reflectance and atmosphere;
  they do not replace the shared chassis palette.
- For screenshots and top-down readability, preserve large value blocks under
  ACES exposure. Thin emissive lines should remain visible at 1280×720 but not
  overpower the silhouette.

## Reference translation

The target combines the bold gameplay silhouettes and material confidence of
hero shooter environments, the bright vertical urban energy of futuristic
cityscapes, and the crisp color blocking of competitive tactical spaces. The
kit must remain an original Axiom Relay language: borrow principles—clarity,
contrast, modularity, and graphic landmarks—not logos, exact props, UI, or
recognizable branded geometry.
